How it works

From requirements to report
in five steps.

DevPath doesn't just throw popular frameworks at you. It walks through your actual situation — what you're building, who's building it, and what you care about most — before making any judgment. Every recommendation is justified, scored, and compared head-to-head.

01
Describe your project type
Pick from 8 categories: Web App, Mobile, API/Backend, E-Commerce, Real-time, AI/ML Platform, Data Platform, or CMS. Each category unlocks different evaluation criteria — a real-time chat app is scored very differently from a data analytics platform.
02
Set your expected scale
Choose from MVP (under 1K users) up to Large Scale (1M+ global users). Scale is one of the most decisive factors in stack selection — a stack perfect for a startup prototype can become a serious liability at 500K users. DevPath accounts for this from the start.
03
Define your team composition
Solo developer, small generalist team, specialized roles, or enterprise? Team structure dramatically narrows what's realistic. A solo dev shouldn't adopt a microservices architecture; an enterprise team has different governance and tooling requirements entirely.
04
Pick your top priorities
Select up to 3: Fast development, High performance, Scalability, Low cost/OSS, Developer experience, Security & compliance, Large hiring pool, Long-term stability, or Rich ecosystem. These aren't just labels — they are directly weighted in the scoring rubric that Claude uses to rank each stack.
05
Get your full ranked report
In about 15 seconds, you receive 3 fully ranked stacks — each with a complete layer breakdown (frontend, backend, database, auth, infra), scores across 5 dimensions, honest weaknesses, head-to-head comparisons between #1 and #2/#3, and a final verdict that references your exact priorities by name.
